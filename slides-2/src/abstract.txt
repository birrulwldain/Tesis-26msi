An Informer-Based Approach for Calibration-Free LIBS Spectral Identification: A Case Study for Emission Spectra of Aceh Traditional Women’s Medicine	Birrul Walidain1, Nasrullah Idris1*, Khairun Saddami2 and Natasya Yuza11Department of Physics, Faculty of Mathematics and Natural Sciences, Universitas Syiah Kuala, Banda Aceh, Indonesia, 231112Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, Universitas Syiah Kuala, Banda Aceh, Indonesia, 23111*Corresponding author: nasrullah.idris@usk.ac.idAbstractAccurate elemental identification in Laser-Induced Breakdown Spectroscopy (LIBS) is hindered by the physical complexity of its spectra, including severe interference from overlapping lines and matrix effects. Moreover, the high-resolution of LIBS spectra poses a significant computational bottleneck for standard deep learning models due to their quadratic complexity. To address this, we propose a predictive analysis method using the Informer architecture, an efficient Transformer variant that leverages ProbSparse Self-Attention to reduce computational complexity to O(LlogL). The model was trained exclusively on a comprehensive, physics-based synthetic dataset modeling plasma conditions via the Saha-Boltzmann equations, using a MultiLabelFocalLoss function to handle imbalanced spectral features. Our evaluation shows that a streamlined 2-layer Informer architecture achieved an optimal macro F1-Score of 0.6445, significantly outperforming a deeper 3-layer model. While the model demonstrates high sensitivity (high recall), its precision is challenged by heavily overlapping signals, reflecting the inherent physical difficulty of the task. The model's practical applicability was demonstrated in a targeted case study analyzing the elemental composition of traditional women's medicine from Banda Aceh. The trained Informer successfully performed a detailed spectral deconvolution, identifying major organic elements (C, N, O), minor nutritional minerals (Ca, Mg), and trace elements (Al, Fe, Mn, Na) from the complex herbal matrix. This nuanced identification highlights the model's sensitivity and potential for real-world applications. This approach validates the Informer-based method as a rapid, calibration-free screening tool, bridging advanced computational models with public health needs.Keywords: Laser-Induced Breakdown Spectroscopy (LIBS); Informer Model; Spectral Identification; Calibration-Free Analysis; Traditional Medicine Analysis.Select a parallel session class you wish to present this title at:?
